Tips for Financial Cybersecurity

1. Secure Your Access Points

The first line of defense is a strong, unique password. Avoid using easily guessable information like birthdays or common words. Instead, use a passphrase—a string of random words that is long but memorable. Most importantly, enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) on all financial accounts. This adds a second layer of security, requiring a code from your phone or an app, ensuring that even if a hacker steals your password, they cannot access your funds.

2. Recognize and Avoid Phishing

Cyber-criminals often use "phishing" emails or SMS messages to trick you into revealing sensitive data. These messages often create a sense of urgency, claiming your account is locked or a suspicious transaction has occurred. Never click on links or download attachments from unexpected sources. If you receive a suspicious request, do not use the contact information provided in the message. Instead, visit the official bank website or use the verified app to check your account status.

3. Monitor and Maintain Digital Hygiene

Regularly reviewing your bank statements and transaction history is vital for spotting unauthorized activity early. Set up real-time alerts for large purchases or password changes. Additionally, ensure your devices are secure by keeping your operating system and banking apps updated; these updates often contain "patches" for security vulnerabilities. Finally, avoid conducting financial business on public Wi-Fi networks, as these are easily intercepted by bad actors.
